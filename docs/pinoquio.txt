Chapter 1 – The Talking Piece of Wood
Mastro Cherry, an old carpenter, finds a plain piece of wood in his workshop. When he tries to work on it, he hears a tiny voice asking him to be careful. Shocked, he attempts to hit and throw the wood but realizes it might be alive. The piece of wood seems to giggle and speak, leaving Mastro Cherry terrified and astonished.

Chapter 2 – Geppetto and the Marionette
Mastro Cherry’s friend Geppetto visits, asking for a piece of wood to carve a marionette that can dance and perform tricks. After a comical exchange of insults and wigs between the two old men, Geppetto receives the wood and heads home, planning to create the puppet.

Chapter 3 – Pinocchio is Born
Geppetto carves the marionette and names him Pinocchio. The puppet shows signs of life, moving eyes and nose, laughing, and being mischievous. Pinocchio’s first prank is stealing Geppetto’s wig. Geppetto teaches him to walk, but Pinocchio escapes into the street, causing a commotion, and is finally caught by a Carabineer who returns him to Geppetto.

Chapter 4 – The Talking Cricket
Pinocchio encounters a Talking Cricket in his home, who tries to warn him about the consequences of being disobedient and lazy. Pinocchio dismisses the cricket’s advice and angrily kills it with a hammer, showing his stubbornness and disregard for guidance.

Chapter 5 – Pinocchio’s Hunger and the Flying Egg
Pinocchio becomes extremely hungry and searches for food. He finds an egg and attempts to cook it, only for a tiny chick to emerge and fly away. Pinocchio realizes the truth of the cricket’s warning about his misbehavior, regretting running away and disobeying Geppetto.

Chapter 6 – Pinocchio Burns His Feet
In his desperation, Pinocchio runs out into the dark, stormy night to seek food. He returns home exhausted, puts his feet on a stove to dry, and falls asleep. During the night, his wooden feet are burned to ashes, setting up the next challenge for Geppetto.

Chapter 7 – Geppetto Shares His Breakfast
Geppetto returns and finds Pinocchio in distress over his burned feet. Misunderstanding some of Pinocchio’s frantic explanations, he sees only the Marionette’s hunger. He kindly shares his breakfast of three pears, peeling them and letting Pinocchio eat the skins and cores, teaching a lesson in gratitude and resourcefulness.

Chapter 8 – New Feet and the ABC Book
After quelling Pinocchio’s complaints, Geppetto makes him new feet using glue and wood. Pinocchio insists on going to school and needs an ABC book, but neither has money. Geppetto sells his coat to buy the book, showing his dedication and love, and Pinocchio appreciates the sacrifice with joy.

Chapter 9 – Pinocchio Sells the ABC Book
Excited about school, Pinocchio is distracted by the sound of a Marionette theater. Tempted by curiosity and fun, he decides to sell his newly bought ABC book to pay for admission, showing early signs of impulsiveness and disregard for his education and Geppetto’s sacrifices.

Chapter 10 – The Marionette Theater
Pinocchio enters the Marionette theater, where his puppet siblings recognize him with cheers. The Director, a terrifying figure, spots Pinocchio and plans to cook him, mistaking him for ordinary wood. Pinocchio’s life is endangered, highlighting his naivety and the dangers of his adventurous spirit.

Chapter 11 – Pinocchio Saves Harlequin
At the Marionette Theater, the terrifying yet kind-hearted Fire Eater feels pity for Pinocchio and sneezes—a sign of his compassion. Pinocchio bravely offers to take Harlequin’s place on the fire, moving everyone with his courage. Fire Eater is touched, pardons Harlequin, and praises Pinocchio. The Marionettes celebrate with dancing and singing through the night.

Chapter 12 – The Fox and the Cat
Fire Eater rewards Pinocchio with five gold coins for his father, Geppetto. On his way home, Pinocchio meets a lame Fox and a blind Cat, who tempt him with the promise of turning his coins into thousands at the “Field of Wonders.” Despite initially refusing, Pinocchio is persuaded by their persuasive trickery and agrees to follow them, abandoning his homeward path.

Chapter 13 – The Inn of the Red Lobster
Exhausted, Pinocchio, the Fox, and the Cat stop at the Inn of the Red Lobster. The Fox and Cat pretend to eat modestly, while Pinocchio worries about the Field of Wonders. The Fox and Cat sneak away before midnight, leaving Pinocchio alone to continue the journey in the dark. On the road, he encounters the ghostly Talking Cricket, who warns him to return home and give his gold to his father, but Pinocchio stubbornly refuses.

Chapter 14 – Pinocchio and the Assassins
Ignoring the Cricket’s advice, Pinocchio is ambushed by two Assassins demanding his gold. He hides the coins under his tongue, bites off a cat’s paw that attacks him, and flees. After a long chase through forests, rivers, and fields, Pinocchio barely escapes but the Assassins remain in pursuit, soaking wet but determined.

Chapter 15 – Pinocchio is Hanged
Exhausted and cornered, Pinocchio seeks refuge in a cottage but finds it abandoned. The Assassins catch him, tie his hands, and hang him from a giant oak. They leave him to die, confident he cannot escape. Pinocchio, weak and terrified, thinks of his father as death approaches.

Chapter 16 – The Lovely Maiden with Azure Hair Rescues Pinocchio
The kind Fairy (the Lovely Maiden with Azure Hair) sees Pinocchio hanging and sends a Falcon to free him. She places him gently in a bed and calls three doctors—the Crow, the Owl, and the Talking Cricket—to examine him. Each gives contradictory opinions about his state, but Pinocchio slowly awakens, sobbing, realizing he is still alive.

Chapter 17 – Pinocchio Lies, His Nose Grows
The Fairy tries to give Pinocchio medicine to cure his fever, but he resists, preferring sugar. When four black Rabbits arrive to claim him, he finally drinks the medicine and recovers. Pinocchio lies to the Fairy about losing the gold coins, causing his nose to grow longer and longer with each lie, teaching him a painful lesson about dishonesty.

Chapter 18 – Pinocchio Meets the Fox and Cat Again
The Fairy reduces Pinocchio’s nose with the help of woodpeckers. She offers him to stay with her and promises his father will come to him. Pinocchio sets off to meet his father but encounters the Fox and Cat again. They remind him of the Field of Wonders and the potential to multiply his coins, tempting him once more away from home.


Chapter 19 – Pinocchio Loses His Gold and Goes to Prison
Pinocchio eagerly returns to the Field of Wonders to retrieve the gold pieces he buried, dreaming of riches and luxuries. However, a parrot reveals that the Fox and the Cat have stolen his coins. Desperate, Pinocchio reports the theft to a monkey judge, who surprisingly sentences him to four months in prison. By luck, during the city’s celebratory release of prisoners after a royal victory, Pinocchio is freed and escapes, relieved to leave jail behind.

Chapter 20 – The Serpent and the Trap
Overjoyed at his freedom, Pinocchio sets out to return to the Fairy. The muddy road and bad weather make the journey difficult, but he resolves to be obedient and good. Along the way, he encounters a giant green serpent blocking the path. After a tense and comedic struggle, the serpent dies from laughter. Continuing his journey, Pinocchio, driven by hunger, tries to pick grapes but falls into a trap set by a farmer.

Chapter 21 – Pinocchio Becomes a Watchdog
Pinocchio is captured by the farmer, who mistakes him for a thief and forces him to serve as a watchdog for his chickens. Chained with a collar and left in a doghouse, Pinocchio suffers from cold, hunger, and fear. Reflecting on his past disobedience, he begins to understand the consequences of his misbehavior and prepares to be patient through the night.

Chapter 22 – Pinocchio Proves His Loyalty
During the night, four weasels attempt to steal the farmer’s chickens. Pinocchio cleverly traps them and alerts the farmer by barking, fulfilling his role as a faithful watchdog. The farmer praises Pinocchio’s honesty and courage, rewarding him by removing the dog collar and granting him his freedom.

Chapter 23 – Pinocchio Searches for Geppetto
After regaining his freedom, Pinocchio learns that the Fairy with Azure Hair has died from grief. Distraught, he meets a giant pigeon who offers to carry him to the seashore. There, Pinocchio spots his father, Geppetto, struggling in a small boat on a rough sea. Seeing the peril, Pinocchio bravely dives into the water to save him, disappearing into the waves as worried onlookers watch.

Chapter 24 – The Island of the Busy Bees
Pinocchio swims all night and reaches a remote island, exhausted but alive. He asks a friendly dolphin for guidance, learning that his father may have been swallowed by a massive shark. Arriving at the Land of the Busy Bees, Pinocchio, starving, struggles to find food. Eventually, a kind woman offers him a task, which he accepts, and he joyfully eats the meal she provides. To his astonishment, he realizes she resembles the Fairy with Azure Hair.

Chapter 25 – Pinocchio Promises to Be Good
Pinocchio discovers that the woman is indeed the Fairy with Azure Hair. He pledges to obey her, study, work, and become a good boy. The Fairy explains the importance of education, labor, and honesty, warning him against laziness. Pinocchio, inspired by her words and tired of being a Marionette, solemnly promises to follow her guidance and hopes to become a real boy.

Chapter 26 – Pinocchio Goes to the Seashore
Pinocchio starts school, but the other boys laugh at him and play tricks. At first, he remains calm, but eventually he defends himself, earning their respect. He studies diligently and earns praise from his teacher and the Fairy. However, he makes many friends, including mischievous boys, who try to lure him away from school. One day, they talk about a huge Shark near the shore and tempt him to go see it. Pinocchio decides to wait until after school, but ends up racing with them to the shore, unaware of the danger his disobedience will bring.

Chapter 27 – The Fight and Pinocchio’s Arrest
At the shore, Pinocchio realizes the Shark was a trick played by the boys. They try to force him to abandon school and enjoy mischief with them. A fight breaks out; Pinocchio defends himself with his wooden strength. During the chaos, one boy is accidentally injured by a thrown book. The other boys flee, leaving Pinocchio with the wounded child. Soon, two Carabineers arrive, and despite Pinocchio’s protest of innocence, they take him to the village for questioning. On the way, a gust of wind blows off his cap, prompting a chase where Pinocchio runs faster than the Carabineers’ dog, Alidoro.

Chapter 28 – Pinocchio Almost Fried
During the chase, Pinocchio narrowly escapes Alidoro by jumping into the sea. Out of kindness, he saves the dog from drowning, earning his gratitude. While swimming near the shore, he gets caught in a fisherman’s net filled with fish. The fisherman, a grotesque green man, mistakes Pinocchio for a fish and prepares to cook him with the others. Pinocchio protests, but the fisherman ignores him, planning to fry him along with the fish. Pinocchio realizes how badly he has suffered due to disobedience and poor choices.

Chapter 29 – Return to the Fairy
Pinocchio’s hope seems lost until Alidoro rescues him from the fisherman and brings him safely back. Grateful, Pinocchio learns that the boy he injured earlier is alive. He acquires makeshift clothing from an old man and returns home, anxious about the Fairy’s reaction. He struggles to reach her house, getting his foot stuck in the door, and endures the night in discomfort. The Snail brings him food, though it is fake. Eventually, Pinocchio faints from exhaustion and fear. When he wakes, the Fairy forgives him again. Pinocchio studies diligently for the rest of the year, earns top marks, and the Fairy promises that tomorrow he will become a real boy, planning a celebration with coffee-and-milk and toast.

Chapter 30 – Pinocchio Runs Away to the Land of Toys
Despite the Fairy’s instructions, Pinocchio becomes tempted when his mischievous friend Lamp-Wick talks about the Land of Toys, a place with no school or study, where children play all year. Initially, Pinocchio resists, wanting to keep his promise to the Fairy. Lamp-Wick insists, describing a carefree life. Pinocchio hesitates, torn between obedience and the lure of endless fun. The chapter sets up his eventual decision to run away, showing the ongoing tension between temptation and responsibility.

Chapter 31 – The Land of Toys
After five months of play, Pinocchio wakes to a big surprise: the wagon to the Land of Toys has arrived. It’s drawn by 24 donkeys wearing leather shoes, driven by a small, fat, charming man. Boys from everywhere crowd inside, eager to escape schools and books. Lamp-Wick jumps on top of the wagon, urging Pinocchio to come, but Pinocchio initially refuses, wanting to return home and study. Eventually, tempted by the promise of endless fun, Pinocchio joins them. After a shaky start mounting a donkey, aided by the Little Man, the journey begins. Along the way, a quiet voice warns Pinocchio that abandoning school and study leads to misfortune. Upon arrival, the Land of Toys is chaotic: streets filled with games, noise, parades, and no school, where boys live only for fun. Pinocchio and Lamp-Wick enjoy themselves, but Pinocchio senses that trouble looms.

Chapter 32 – Donkey Ears
The next morning, Pinocchio discovers a terrible surprise: his ears have grown into long donkey ears. In panic, he consults a Dormouse, who informs him that this is “donkey fever,” and soon he will transform into a real donkey—a punishment for lazy boys who abandon school and work. Pinocchio blames Lamp-Wick for persuading him to go to the Land of Toys. Searching for his friend, he finds Lamp-Wick, who is also hiding under a bag. When they finally remove their caps to see each other, they realize they share the same fate. They begin laughing at the situation, but the laughter turns to horror as their bodies start transforming: they grow donkey tails, snouts, and gray hair, ultimately braying as real donkeys. A knock at the door announces the return of the Little Man.

Chapter 33 – Pinocchio Sold
Pinocchio, now a donkey, is taken by the Little Man to be sold. Lamp-Wick is bought by a farmer, while Pinocchio is sold to a circus master who trains him for tricks. Training is harsh and painful; it takes three months of lashes and labor for Pinocchio to perform. On his first public appearance, the audience is thrilled, but Pinocchio spots the Fairy and tries to call her—only brays come out. Attempting to leap through a ring, he injures himself and becomes lame. The circus manager, deeming him useless, sells him to a man who wants only his skin to make a drumhead. Pinocchio is thrown into the sea with a stone around his neck, facing imminent death.

Chapter 34 – Pinocchio and the Terrible Shark
Pinocchio, after being thrown into the sea as a donkey, is eaten by fish but miraculously returns to his wooden Marionette form. He explains to his former master his adventures: how his laziness and bad companions turned him into a donkey, his lameness from the circus, and how the Fairy with the Azure Hair protected him from drowning. After teasing his master, Pinocchio swims away and heads toward a white rock where a strange blue-haired little Goat calls him. On his way, the enormous Terrible Shark appears, swallows him, and Pinocchio finds himself inside its dark, enormous stomach. There he meets a Tunny fish, and together they plan to survive the peril.

Chapter 35 – Inside the Shark
Inside the Shark, Pinocchio discovers his father, Geppetto, who had been swallowed two years earlier. Geppetto has survived thanks to supplies from a sunken ship also eaten by the Shark. Pinocchio reassures his father and proposes a daring escape by climbing the Shark’s throat. They attempt to exit while the Shark sleeps, but a sneeze throws them back inside. Undeterred, they try again, with Pinocchio carrying Geppetto on his shoulders, determined to reach safety.

Chapter 36 – Pinocchio Becomes a Real Boy
Pinocchio and Geppetto escape the Shark with the help of the Tunny, who carries them safely to shore. They find their way to a straw cottage where the Talking Cricket lives. Pinocchio helps his father recover, works hard, studies diligently, and earns money to support both himself and Geppetto. When he returns to check on the Fairy with the Azure Hair, he learns she is ill, so he gives her the fifty pennies he saved. After continuing his good deeds, Pinocchio awakens one morning to find he has transformed into a real boy. His father is restored as well, and their home is filled with joy. Pinocchio rejoices at leaving his Marionette life behind.
